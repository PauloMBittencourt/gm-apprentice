# Vampire: The Masquerade 20th Anniversary -- Session Procedures

Procedural guidance for running V20 chronicles. Supplements the
high-level checklists in `gm-session-patterns.md` with V20-specific
procedures: chronicle building, the prelude, hunting scenes, hunger,
morality pacing, Kindred politics between sessions, and experience.

## Core Mechanical Reminders

- **Dice:** pool of d10s (Attribute + Ability) against a difficulty
  (usually 6). Each 1 cancels a success; no successes and at least one
  1 is a botch. Details in `mechanics.md`.
- **Willpower:** 1 point = 1 automatic success (not on degeneration or
  Virtue-driven rolls where the book forbids it).
- **Blood:** spend to heal, raise Physical Attributes, power
  Disciplines, and rise each night. The per-turn limit depends on
  Generation (`mechanics.md`).
- **Damage:** bashing, lethal, aggravated. Vampires halve bashing,
  soak lethal with Stamina, and soak aggravated only with Fortitude
  (`rules-reference.md`).
- **The Beast:** frenzy (Self-Control) and Rötschreck (Courage);
  degeneration (Conscience, difficulty 8) when a sin on the character's
  Hierarchy is committed (`humanity-paths.md`).
- **Abilities and dramatic systems:** `skills.md`.

## Building a Chronicle

V20 chronicles usually have a beginning, middle and end. Build them
in this order:

1. **Start with the players.** Agree on the kind of game and the kind
   of characters before fixing your concept; don't force a concept on
   anyone.
2. **Make characters together with the city.** Brainstorm backgrounds
   with each player and mine their Allies, Contacts, Resources, Herd
   and Retainers for NPCs and conflicts.
3. **Set the World of Darkness tone.** The book's four touchstones:
   virtue is extinct, life is cheap and violent, there are no heroes
   left, and the city is decaying around its Gothic landmarks. Dial
   them up or down to taste.
4. **Pick the city and its landmarks.** A major city works best. Give
   key sites a symbolic job (an abandoned station as Elysium).
5. **Choose a theme or two** (an age-old war, a conspiracy, the loss of
   humanity) and outline the chronicle from first night to climax.
6. **Cast the Storyteller characters** the outline needs.

*Source: V20 pp. 345-351*

### Storyteller Characters

For each important NPC decide, in this order: their **role** in the
story, a **picture** (one or two vivid features), a **name** that fits
the mood, their **age** (older Kindred are further from mortal life),
one or two **personality** words, a **past** with loves and grudges,
a **quirk**, a **weakness** (villains without flaws are boring), and
only then **statistics**. NPCs don't need to follow player-character
creation rules.

*Source: V20 pp. 351-352*

### The Prelude

A one-on-one mini-session with each player, played quickly from the
character's mortal life to the Embrace. It sets up their ties to the
living and gives you NPCs. Questions the book suggests covering:

- What was unique about their childhood?
- What kind of person were they?
- How did the Embrace change them?
- Who was their sire, and how were they treated?
- Where is their haven?
- Where do they usually feed?
- What drives them now?

A longer version, **boot camp**, has the whole troupe play mortals
for a full story arc before the Embrace, which makes the loss hit
harder.

*Source: V20 pp. 352-354, 363-364*

## Typical V20 Session Flow

A session is a **chapter**; several chapters make a **story**.

1. **Dusk** (5-10 min): the characters rise. Ask who is hungry, check
   blood pools, and deliver any message or summons left during the day.
2. **Hook** (10-20 min): a summons to court, a favour called in, a
   body that breaks the Masquerade, a mortal tie in trouble.
3. **Investigation and intrigue** (60-90 min): Elysium, informants,
   havens, hunting grounds. Social scenes carry as much weight as
   fights.
4. **Complication** (15-30 min): an elder's agenda surfaces, a hunter
   gets close, the Beast slips loose, a boon is called in.
5. **Confrontation** (30-45 min): violent or political. Remember that
   a public fight is a Masquerade problem.
6. **Before dawn** (10-15 min): find shelter, count the cost, ask about
   Humanity, and award experience.

The sun is a hard clock: every scene happens between dusk and dawn,
and being caught out is lethal.

## Hunting Scenes

Pick the method that fits the moment:

| Method | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| **One roll** | Busy session; the blood just needs to be had | The hunting roll by area (`skills.md`, "Dramatic Systems") |
| **Three-stage hunt** | To make feeding matter, or to fill a quiet stretch | **Contact** (find prey, usually Perception), **hunt** (get them alone: Subterfuge, Stealth, Animal Ken...), **defeat** (feed; the vampire can take no more blood points than successes). Failure at any stage and the prey escapes or raises the alarm |
| **Narrated** | The player wants blood without dice | No roll; the vampire gets the blood, but something goes wrong as if they had botched |

**Keep the others involved:** let other players voice the victim and
bystanders, based on the hunter's rolls.

**Flashes of life:** for each success on the hunting (or final-stage)
roll, give the player one image from the victim's life as the blood
leaves them -- a broken toy, a beach with a spouse, a hospital
bedside. Match the images to the feeding style (violent memories for
an intimidating hunt, awkward intimacy for a seduction). Keep a list
ready.

*Source: V20 pp. 365-366*

## Hunger

- Rule of thumb: a vampire is **hungry** when its blood pool is below
  **7 minus Self-Control (or Instinct)**.
- Give each player a two-sided card, **Hungry / Satiated**, and flip
  it when they cross the line.
- When a session lulls, ask "is anyone hungry?" A hungry vampire is a
  story about to happen.
- Hunger tightens frenzy: blood becomes a trigger, and Self-Control
  rolls can't use more dice than the blood pool holds
  (`mechanics.md`, "Hunger").

*Source: V20 p. 366*

## Morality Pacing

- Warn before a sin that would call for a degeneration roll, then
  roll if the player goes ahead.
- One or two meaningful checks per session hit harder than many.
- Make the character's Hierarchy of Sins visible at the table.
- Describe the effects of low Humanity: mortals flinch, animals
  bolt, the face in the mirror (if any) looks wrong.

Full rules: `humanity-paths.md`.

## Storytelling Techniques

| Technique | What it does | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Downtime** ("blue-booking") | A player narrates off-screen actions to you between sessions, in writing or one-on-one; good for secrets, backstory and adult material | Agree with the troupe whether downtimes are shared |
| **Cut scenes** | Jump between two simultaneous scenes that contrast (an Embrace intercut with memories of home) | Hard to run without losing pace |
| **Flashbacks** | Play a past moment in full, to reveal something or deliver an epiphany | Tie it to the chronicle's theme |
| **Dream sequences** | Symbolic dreams that magnify mistakes or show Merits and Flaws | Keep them short and strange |
| **Parallel stories** | Players run other characters (ghouls, hunters, the coterie's mortal ties) in a side plot | Lets them see the monsters their main characters have become |
| **Boot camp** | Play the characters as mortals for a whole arc before the Embrace | Takes sessions; worth it for long chronicles |

*Source: V20 pp. 361-364*

## The Book's Ten Pieces of Advice

**Do:** involve the players and their backstories; find out what game
they want; prepare at least some notes; put story before rules; bring
scenes alive with description, voices and action.

**Don't:** fill the city with cardboard stereotypes; deny a fair
payoff to good play; explain everything; use your authority to force
the plot; panic -- call a break when players surprise you.

*Source: V20 pp. 360-361*

## Social and Political Scenes

- **Elysium is a stage:** a place (opera house, museum, theatre) where
  Kindred meet without fear of harm, and everyone is watching. Many
  Princes add their own rules. Use Harpies and gossip to make
  reputations move.
- **Boons are the currency.** Note who owes whom after each session
  (`sects.md`, "Boons").
- **Social rolls support roleplay, they don't replace it.** Let the
  player argue, then roll to see how well it lands.
- **Mind-control Disciplines have social costs.** Dominating someone
  at court is a crime if discovered.

## Combat Procedures

- **Three stages per turn:** initiative, attack, resolution
  (`rules-reference.md`). Declare in reverse initiative order, act in
  initiative order.
- **Kindred fights are short and ugly.** Most end when one side flees
  or frenzies, or a stake goes in.
- **The Masquerade is a combatant:** witnesses, cameras, police and
  sirens arrive. A fight that drags on in public is a story about the
  cover-up.
- **Mortal mooks** get four health levels and break quickly
  (`rules-reference.md`).
- **Fire and sunlight** cause Rötschreck; use them sparingly and
  meaningfully.

## Faction Turn (V20-Specific)

Called by `world-evolution.md` Step 2 during post-session updates.
Overrides Step 4 of the Universal Faction Turn with V20 pacing.

V20 has **no clocks or tracks**; factions advance through the fiction.
Between sessions, for each active faction (the Prince's court, a
clan, the Anarchs, a Sabbat pack, a hunter cell, an elder with a
scheme), ask:

1. **What did they want this week?** One concrete move: claim a
   domain, call in a boon, plant a ghoul, frame a rival, recruit.
2. **What did the coterie's actions change?** Any boon gained or owed,
   any Tradition broken in public, any NPC humiliated or saved.
3. **Who noticed?** Kindred society runs on gossip. Decide which
   Harpy, Nosferatu or Sheriff heard about it, and what it costs.
4. **Did the Masquerade slip?** A breach draws mortal attention
   (police, press, hunters) and the Prince's attention, in that order.

**Heat with the authorities (narrative, not numeric):**

- **Unnoticed:** no one cares what the coterie does.
- **Watched:** a Hound, a ghoul or a Nosferatu is keeping tabs.
- **Summoned:** the Prince, Baron or Archbishop wants a word, and a
  favour.
- **Hunted:** a blood hunt, an Archon or a hunter cell.

Move at most one step per session unless the coterie does something
flagrant.

**Mortal ties:** each session, check one character's mortal
connection (family, old friend, Herd). It thrives, is threatened, or
finds out something it shouldn't. This keeps the Humanity theme alive.

## Experience

At the end of each **chapter** (session), award 1-5 points:

| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 1 | Automatic -- everyone gets it |
| 1 | **Learning curve:** the player says what the character learned, and you agree |
| 1 (2 if inspired) | **Roleplaying** that was apt, not just entertaining |
| 1 | **Heroism:** risking everything to save others, and surviving. Don't reward stupidity |

At the end of a **story**, a few bonus points:

| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Success:** goals achieved, even partly |
| 1 | **Danger:** survived serious odds |
| 1 | **Wisdom:** a plan that saved the coterie |

**Spending:**

- The Storyteller approves where points go; they should reflect what
  happened in play.
- **No Trait rises more than one dot per story.**
- **New Abilities and Disciplines need a teacher or a reason**; finding
  one is a story in itself.
- **Backgrounds** aren't bought with experience; they come from play.
- **Raising a Virtue** doesn't raise Humanity or Willpower.
- Costs are in `character-generation.md`.

*Source: V20 pp. 122-124*

## Arc Drivers

**Arc fuel:** losing Humanity one choice at a time; mortal ties that
the Embrace is slowly destroying; a sire's or elder's agenda the
character is caught in; debts (boons, blood bonds) that come due; the
Jyhad using the coterie as pawns.

**Per-session touchpoints:** one scene that tests a character's
Humanity or Path; one mortal connection on screen; one move in Kindred
politics that affects the coterie; one hunt or feeding that says
something about who the character is becoming.
