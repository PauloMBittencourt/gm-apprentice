# Vampire: The Masquerade 20th Anniversary -- Sects, Traditions and Titles

Kindred politics in V20: the sects, the Six Traditions, the titles
that run a domain, and boons. Use it to build a city's power
structure, to cast NPCs, and to judge how a domain reacts when
characters break its laws.

Clan-to-sect alignment is in `clans.md`. Blood bonds, the Vaulderie
and diablerie are in `rules-reference.md`. Paths of Enlightenment
(common in the Sabbat) are in `humanity-paths.md`.

## Quick Reference

| Sect | Core belief | Ruler of a city | Typical strongholds (book) | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Camarilla | Keep the Masquerade; the Traditions are law; elders rule | Prince | Chicago, New Orleans, Vienna, London, Paris | 38-40 |
| Sabbat | Gehenna is coming; vampires should rule mortals, not hide; freedom and loyalty to the Sword | Archbishop (or council of Bishops) | Detroit, Miami, Mexico City, Montreal, Madrid | 40-43 |
| Anarch Movement | Power by merit, not age; take it back from the elders | Baron | Los Angeles, San Diego, San Francisco | 43-45 |
| Independents | Stay out of the Camarilla-Sabbat war; clan agendas first | Clan elders | Alamut (Assamite), Cairo (Setite), Venice (Giovanni), Kolkata (Ravnos) | 46-47 |
| Inconnu | Elders seeking Golconda; they watch, rarely act | -- | Rarely more than one or two per city | 21 |
| Tal'Mahe'Ra | The "True Black Hand": a paranoid sect based in the Underworld | -- | No mortal cities | 21 |

A vampire's sect is a choice only on paper: anyone living in a Sabbat
city is Sabbat whether they like it or not, and anyone ignoring a
Prince in a Camarilla city needs a very good excuse.

*Source: V20 pp. 20-21, 38-47*

## The Camarilla

**Nicknames:** the Ivory Tower. Members call themselves **Kindred**.

Founded in the 15th century to survive the Inquisition. It claims to
*be* vampire society and is the largest sect, present in most cities.
In practice its members come mainly from the seven founding clans:
Brujah, Gangrel, Malkavian, Nosferatu, Toreador, Tremere and Ventrue.
Those clans each have a seat on the Inner Circle; everyone else is
tolerated or ignored.

**At the table:**

- **Court:** most Princes hold regular court, part party and part
  trial, where Kindred gossip, scheme and seek favour.
- **Blood hunt (Lextalionis):** for the worst crimes -- a flagrant
  Masquerade breach, diablerie, insulting the Prince. The Prince
  declares it at court, word passes through the Primogen, and every
  Kindred who hears must take part, even if only by staying out of the
  way. Many Princes pardon whoever diablerises the target.
- **Conclaves:** called only by a Justicar; any Kindred the Camarilla
  claims can attend and bring grievances. They declare wars, call
  blood hunts, reinterpret the Traditions and occasionally "retire" a
  Prince.
- **Modern tension:** elders grow paranoid about Sabbat spies and
  Anarch sympathisers; neonates feel like serfs but understand modern
  technology far better than their elders.

*Source: V20 pp. 38-40*

## The Sabbat

**Nicknames:** the Sword of Caine. Members call themselves
**Cainites** and despise the word "Kindred".

Believes Gehenna is near and the Antediluvians will rise to devour
their descendants. Vampires are superior to mortals and should rule
them, not hide. Many Sabbat give up Humanity for a Path of
Enlightenment. Their cities are violent, and the Masquerade there
(the "Silence of the Blood") is thin.

**Structure and practice:**

- **The pack** is the core unit, bound by the **Vinculum** through the
  Vaulderie (`rules-reference.md`). A Cainite without a pack is
  suspect.
- **Freedom and loyalty** are the two principles, and they often
  conflict.
- **Antitribu:** apart from Lasombra and Tzimisce, most Sabbat see
  themselves as the "anti-clans" of their parent clans (bloodlines
  chapter, `clans.md`).
- **Creation Rites** test a recruit and often strip away Humanity.
  Wartime **shovelheads** are Embraced, knocked out and buried to
  dig themselves out.
- **Ritae:** rituals that parody the Catholic Church, including the
  Vaulderie. Samples are in the book's appendix (p. 507).
- **Factions:** the **Black Hand** (an elite militia, marked by a
  sigil on the right palm), the **Loyalists** (every vampire their
  own master, orders be damned) and the **Status Quo**.

### The Code of Milan (Paraphrased)

The Sabbat's law, with three later addenda:

1. Stay united behind the Regent, who guarantees freedom.
2. Serve your leaders while they serve the Regent.
3. Observe the ritae.
4. Keep your word to fellow Sabbat.
5. Treat peers fairly and provide for brethren in need.
6. Put the Sect before yourself.
7. Those without honour under the Code lose the right to help.
8. The Lextalionis is the model of justice.
9. Protect one another from the Sect's enemies; personal enemies are
   your own problem.
10. Defend Sabbat territory against every other power.
11. Freedom is the founding principle; demand it from your leaders.
12. Settle disputes by **Monomacy** (ritual duel).
13. Support the Black Hand.
14. *(Addendum)* Any Sabbat may watch their fellows to protect freedom
    and security.
15. *(Addendum)* Any Sabbat may call a council of peers and leaders.
16. *(Addendum)* Act, through approved means and a quorum of Prisci,
    against members who abuse their authority for personal gain.

*Source: V20 pp. 40-43*

## The Anarch Movement

Officially a faction inside the Camarilla -- which calls it
protection, while the Anarchs call it oppression. Most Anarchs work
within that structure; only radicals want out entirely. They agree
that power should move from elders to everyone and be earned by
merit, and disagree on almost everything else. Not all are
anarchists: some run domains as democracies, others try neo-feudalism.

- **The Anarch Free State** in California came out of the Second
  Anarch Revolt (1944). Its Revolutionary Council adopted a charter
  of self-government, the **Status Perfectus**, then dissolved.
- **Reputation over rank:** Anarchs dislike formal status. What
  counts is whether others have heard of you and what you've done.
- **Games and pranks** are part of Anarch life, to test nerve and let
  off steam. Some Anarchs think they threaten the Masquerade and the
  cause.
- **Who joins:** not only angry Brujah neonates. The Movement is
  mixed, and many members are thoughtful ancillae.

*Source: V20 pp. 43-45*

## The Independents

The **Assamites**, **Followers of Set**, **Giovanni** and **Ravnos**
are full clans that refuse to fight the Camarilla-Sabbat war. Their
elders are thought to be more active than the Sects' elders, each
with an agenda of its own. Younger members sometimes join a Sect,
and every independent puts their own goals first, so outsiders can
never be sure whose interest one is serving.

*Source: V20 pp. 46-47*

## Outside the Sects

- **Autarkis:** vampires who answer to no Sect, clan or Prince --
  either beneath notice (a Nosferatu in the sewers, a broken
  Malkavian in the Barrens) or too dangerous to bring to heel.
- **Inconnu** and **Tal'Mahe'Ra:** see the Quick Reference. Both are
  best used as distant, mysterious presences.

*Source: V20 pp. 21-22*

## The Six Traditions

The Camarilla treats them as law, with the Prince as their
interpreter and enforcer. Other Sects and domains vary them, but some
authority always makes the rules. Summaries in our own words:

| # | Tradition | Rule | How Princes vary it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Masquerade** | Never reveal your nature to those not of the Blood | The most important; Princes spend influence covering breaches. The Sabbat looks forward to a night it isn't needed |
| 2 | **The Domain** | A vampire's domain is their own; others must respect their word there | From "the whole city is the Prince's" to "every acknowledged Kindred holds their own turf" |
| 3 | **The Progeny** | Embrace only with your elder's permission, or both sire and childe die | Usually the Prince's permission; liberal domains accept a clan elder or one's own sire, and get overcrowded |
| 4 | **The Accounting** | Your childer are yours to command until Released, and their crimes are yours | Some sires make childer earn their freedom; others cut them loose on night one |
| 5 | **Hospitality** | Present yourself to the ruler of any city you enter; without acceptance you have no standing | Some Princes track every arrival; others care only if the order is kept |
| 6 | **Destruction** | Only your elder may destroy one of your kind; only the eldest calls the blood hunt | The blood hunt is the ultimate penalty, used rarely |

*Source: V20 pp. 22-24*

## Titles

Not every domain has every title; a small domain may give one vampire
several, and a big one may have several of the same.

### City Rulers

| Title | Sect | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Prince** | Camarilla | Master of the city: interprets and enforces the Traditions; declares Elysium, calls blood hunts, settles disputes, may claim blood tribute and appoint or ignore the Primogen. Real power lasts only as long as the domain respects it (**praxis**). |
| **Archbishop** | Sabbat | Part spiritual leader, part warlord; less about the Traditions, more about the holy war. Some cities have a council of Bishops instead. |
| **Baron** | Anarch | A Prince in all but name. Always walking the line between wise leader and the autocrat the Anarchs hate. |

### Camarilla

| Title | Role |
|---|---|
| Primogen | The eldest or most influential of each clan, often a council advising the Prince; how they are chosen varies by city |
| Sheriff | The Prince's enforcer; may appoint **Hounds** as deputies |
| Harpy | Arbiter of taste and opinion; becomes Harpy by acting like one rather than by appointment |
| Keeper of Elysium | Looks after the Elysium sites and their customs |
| Scourge | Destroys thin-blooded vampires (14th Generation and higher) in paranoid domains |
| Justicar | One of the Inner Circle's judges; punishes Tradition-breaking on a large scale; serves 13 years, answerable only to another Justicar |
| Archon | A Justicar's agent, chosen for skill more than status; serves at the Justicar's pleasure |
| Alastor | Secret police who hunt the "Red List"; largely immune to local Princes |
| Inner Circle | The eldest of each founding clan, meeting every 13 years to set the Camarilla's course |

### Sabbat

| Title | Role |
|---|---|
| Ductus | Pack leader; mostly honorary, and one who throws their weight around gets dumped |
| Pack Priest | Leads the pack's ritae; second in command and acting leader if the Ductus falls |
| Templar (Paladin) | Elite bodyguard to a Bishop or higher |
| Bishop | Below the Archbishop, in charge of one area (war, spiritual life, hunting infernalists) |
| Priscus | Respected adviser who earns the rank over time, never by appointment |
| Cardinal | Oversees a whole region's Archbishops and its expansion |
| Regent | Supreme leader of the Sabbat, in Mexico City |

*Source: V20 pp. 24-26*

## Boons

The everyday currency of the Camarilla (and of independents who deal
with it) is favours owed and owed back. **Boons** are given, called
in and traded: a debt to one elder can end up being paid to someone
else entirely. A broken boon wrecks reputations. The core book only
sketches the system; size boons by what was done (a minor favour, a
life saved) and keep a ledger.

*Source: V20 p. 39*

## Running Sect Politics at the Table

- **Decide who rules before the session**, and how securely. A weak
  Prince or a Baron facing a rival changes how every NPC behaves.
- **Every title is a hook.** A Sheriff hunting the coterie, a Harpy
  who mocks them at Elysium, a Primogen who wants a favour -- each is
  a scene waiting to happen.
- **The Traditions bend to whoever enforces them.** Ask "who will
  punish this, and do they want to?" rather than "what does the rule
  say?"
- **Track boons.** A short list of who owes whom makes Kindred
  society feel like a web of debts.
- **Sect is attitude, not just membership.** Use the book's "view
  from without" quotes as a guide: each Sect sneers at the others in
  its own way.
